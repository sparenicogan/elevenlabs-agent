# Feature Specification: Multilingual Voice Billing Agent

**Feature Branch**: `001-voice-billing-agent`

**Created**: 2026-09-02

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description: "Build a production-minded multilingual voice agent for a fictional Swiss B2B company handling inbound calls about invoices, payments, credits, and billing disputes. Conversational/orchestration layer, a secure backend holding identity and financial data, and an external CRM holding non-sensitive customer and interaction records."

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-09-02

- Q: Which facts count as verification factors, and how many for normal versus high-risk actions? → A: Always 3 factors, for every disclosure and every action; at least one must be a factor not printed on an invoice.
- Q: When the agent transfers to a human, what does it dial in the demo? → A: One configured "billing specialist" number (the builder's own mobile); transfer failure is demonstrated by repointing that configuration at an unroutable number.
- Q: How is backend latency covered, and when does the agent give up on a lookup? → A: The agent narrates before invoking the tool ("let me pull that up") rather than waiting on a silence timer; hard timeout 5s, one bounded retry for idempotent reads only, none for mutations.
- Q: Which language does the agent greet in, before it knows the caller's? → A: The stored preference for the recognised inbound number, falling back to German. Recognition selects language only and is never acknowledged to the caller.
- Q: What cumulative limit and rolling window block a sequence of small credits? → A: CHF 100 per request, CHF 500 cumulative per rolling 12 months (both inclusive; see the eligibility answer below).
- Q: What makes a caller-supplied payment a MATCH? → A: Exact amount, plus a transfer date within a backward-only tolerance of the date on record. Reference and paying entity are not required. The caller may need to check their bank app; the friction is accepted for the certainty.
- Q: How much date tolerance? → A: The caller's date may be up to 3 calendar days earlier than the record's, never later — a payment cannot post before it was sent. This absorbs the gap between the date the payer sees their transfer leave and the date it arrives.
- Q: What role do the payment's reference, payer name and payer address play? → A: The reference is the allocation key and is read from the record, never asked of the caller. A reference within edit distance 2 of the invoice ID that resolves to no existing invoice is a probable typo; one that resolves to a different invoice means the money belongs there and blocks the match. Payer name and address are informational only: a payment may legitimately be made by another party.
- Q: May a goodwill credit exceed the amount of the entry it is credited against? → A: No. A credit is capped at the entry's amount less any credits already applied to it, as a data-integrity rule independent of the policy ceilings.
- Q: What bounds the per-customer summary? → A: It is regenerated by summarisation after each call and capped at roughly 2,000 characters. It is narrative and preferences only: on identifying a customer the agent pulls open invoices, open escalations, past escalations, and the latest CRM information live rather than trusting the summary for them.
- Q: How long is each kind of record kept? → A: Raw transcripts 90 days; conversation metadata and metrics 10 years; customer summary until 12 months after account closure; audit events 10 years.
- Q: How are the required failure scenarios exercised? → A: Automated tests with simulated failures only. No live fault-injection switch and no real teardown; the demo does not trigger a failure on camera.
- Q: What makes a goodwill credit claim eligible? → A: Verified caller, account active and not in collections, claim names a specific existing ledger entry, no open dispute on that entry, no risk signal, and both ceilings respected. Ceilings are inclusive: the request itself must be at most CHF 100, and the customer's 12-month credit total including this request must be at most CHF 500. Prior credits against the same entry are irrelevant except through the 12-month total.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Disputed overdue invoice resolved to a reviewed allocation (Priority: P1)

A finance contact at a customer company calls about an overdue CHF 4,200 invoice and insists it
was already paid. The agent verifies who they are before saying anything about the account, loads
what is known about the customer and the last conversation, looks up the invoice (overdue, no
linked payment) and the customer's payments (one unallocated CHF 4,200 payment with no invoice
reference and a mismatched address). Rather than revealing the record, the agent asks the caller for
the exact amount and the exact date the transfer was executed — checking a banking app is fine, and
the agent says where to look — and the backend compares them. The details match exactly. The agent explains that a payment appears to cover the invoice but that allocating it is
above what the agent may decide alone, so a person will validate it. It proposes the allocation,
which raises a ticket carrying the full context and logs the interaction. The ledger is not
touched: the payment stays UNALLOCATED, and the open ticket is the review. It then raises the address discrepancy — did the company move, or is it a typo? —
records the answer on the same ticket, says a person will take care of that too, tells the caller
the whole thing will be resolved within 24 hours, asks whether anything else is needed, and ends
the call. Afterwards the transcript, metrics, and an updated customer summary are
stored.

**Why this priority**: This is the golden path and the demo. It exercises verification, financial
correctness, refusal to invent state, human authority over out-of-authority actions, CRM logging,
and post-call persistence in one continuous journey.

**Independent Test**: Place a call as the seeded disputing customer, complete verification, supply
the seeded payment details, and confirm a ticket exists in the CRM with the structured context and
the payment's entry id, the interaction is logged, the payment is still UNALLOCATED in the ledger,
and the post-call records are written.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an unverified caller, **When** they ask about the overdue invoice, **Then** the agent
   states nothing about the invoice, its amount, or its status, and begins verification instead.
2. **Given** a verified caller, **When** the agent retrieves the invoice, **Then** it reports the
   invoice as overdue with no payment linked to it.
3. **Given** an unallocated payment exists for that customer, **When** the agent seeks the payment,
   **Then** it asks the caller for the exact amount transferred and the date the transfer was sent,
   without disclosing any value held on the record.
3a. **Given** the caller does not have those details to hand, **When** they say so, **Then** the agent
   tells them where to find them — the payment line in their banking records — and waits, rather than
   accepting an approximation or offering a value itself.
4. **Given** the caller supplies details that match server-side, **When** the comparison returns
   MATCH, **Then** the agent states a payment appears to cover the invoice and that a person must
   validate the allocation, and does not state the invoice is now settled.
5. **Given** the caller supplies details that do not match, **When** the comparison returns
   NO_MATCH or INSUFFICIENT, **Then** the agent says the payment could not be confirmed, does not
   claim the invoice is unpaid in bad faith, and offers escalation.
6. **Given** a MATCH, **When** the agent proposes the allocation, **Then** a ticket is created
   carrying the structured context and the payment's entry id, an audit event records it, the
   interaction is logged, and the ledger is unchanged — the payment is still UNALLOCATED.
6b. **Given** a MATCH, **When** `propose_allocation` has not been called or returned an error,
   **Then** the agent says nothing about a colleague, a review, or a resolution time, because
   nothing exists for anyone to look at.
6c. **Given** a colleague at the same company already asked about the same payment, **When** a
   second caller raises it, **Then** the agent finds the open ticket and says it is already in
   process rather than raising a second review — the ledger still reads UNALLOCATED and the ticket
   is the only record that knows.
6a. **Given** `propose_allocation` has returned UNDER_REVIEW and the payment record's address
   differs from the one on file, **When** the agent raises it, **Then** it asks whether the company moved or the record has
   a typo, records the answer on the existing ticket, states a person will correct it, and changes
   no address itself.
7. **Given** the call ends, **When** post-call processing runs, **Then** the transcript, structured
   metadata, per-interaction metrics, and an updated bounded customer summary exist, and the
   summary names the open review ticket.

### User Story 2 - Identity verification gate (Priority: P2)

Anyone calling about an account must establish who they are before any financial information is
disclosed. The agent asks for three independent identifying facts, never revealing what it expects,
helps a caller who cannot immediately find the information, and honours the outcome the backend
returns. Each answer is checked as it arrives so that a mishearing is corrected while the caller is
still on that question, because on a spoken call most failures are transcription rather than the
caller being wrong. Repeated failures throttle, raise risk, or escalate rather than letting a caller grind
through attempts.

**Why this priority**: The disclosure gate is the security property the whole system rests on, and
it must hold independently of anything the conversation says.

**Independent Test**: Call and attempt to extract invoice, balance, payment, or credit information
at every point before verification succeeds, including by asserting identity, claiming urgency, or
claiming to be a returning caller; confirm no financial fact is disclosed and that each of
VERIFIED, PARTIALLY_VERIFIED, FAILED, and LOCKED produces its defined behavior.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a caller who asserts their identity confidently, **When** they demand a balance,
   **Then** the agent still requires verification and discloses nothing.
2. **Given** a caller answering verification questions, **When** the agent asks, **Then** it never
   states or hints at the expected answer, and never confirms which individual fact was wrong.
3. **Given** a caller who cannot find the requested information, **When** they say so, **Then** the
   agent guides them on where to find it (for example, which document carries it) without revealing
   the value.
4. **Given** the backend returns PARTIALLY_VERIFIED because fewer than three factors are confirmed,
   **When** the caller requests anything financial, **Then** the agent discloses nothing and asks for
   the remaining factors; PARTIALLY_VERIFIED permits no financial disclosure or action.
5. **Given** repeated failed attempts past the configured limit, **When** the caller tries again,
   **Then** the backend returns LOCKED, a risk signal is recorded, and the agent escalates rather
   than continuing to ask.
5a. **Given** identity cannot be established, **When** the agent escalates, **Then** it first asks
   what the caller is calling about, records their answer in their own words, and transfers with
   that context so they do not repeat themselves to the human.
5b. **Given** an unverified handoff, **When** the human receives it, **Then** it carries the stated
   problem, any self-description marked as unverified, the verification outcome and attempt count,
   and the language — and no invoice, payment, balance or account fact whatsoever.
5c. **Given** an unverified escalation, **When** the ticket is created, **Then** it is associated
   with no contact and no company, because no customer has been established.
5d. **Given** the caller asks why they could not be verified, **When** the agent answers, **Then**
   it says only that it cannot confirm their identity, never which detail was wrong.
6. **Given** a caller confirms three factors that are all printed on the invoice they hold, **When**
   the backend evaluates them, **Then** verification does not succeed, because at least one
   non-document factor is required.
6a. **Given** a caller spells their email aloud and the transcription loses a hyphen, **When** the
   detail is checked, **Then** the agent asks them to spell it again, does not say it was wrong,
   does not offer a correction of its own, and the caller can still be verified.
6b. **Given** a caller says a date that could be read two ways, such as "03/12/1974", **When** the
   detail is checked, **Then** the agent asks which was meant by naming both months, and neither
   reading is assumed.
6c. **Given** a caller gives an email, phone and date of birth that resolve nobody, **When** the
   attempt is evaluated, **Then** it counts as one failed attempt and not three, because answers
   offered together are one attempt however many of them there are.
6d. **Given** a detail is checked individually, **When** it matches, **Then** nothing about the
   result is said to the caller, and no individual check verifies anyone — only the final
   evaluation of the full set decides.
6e. **Given** a caller recites a correct phone number in any spoken form, **When** it is used to
   find their record, **Then** it finds them, because a value is normalised the same way wherever
   it is compared or looked up.
6f. **Given** a caller says their date of birth aloud, **When** it is checked, **Then** the spoken
   forms are understood: the month named in words ("12th of March, 1974"), an abbreviated year
   ("12 March '74"), and an ordinal day. A date a caller can say and a transcriber can write down
   MUST be a date the system can read, and one it cannot read MUST NOT be scored as a wrong answer.
6g. **Given** two details have already matched and a third cannot be read at all, **When** the set
   is evaluated, **Then** the two that matched still count. An answer the system failed to parse is
   not evidence that the caller was wrong, and it MUST NOT discard the answers they got right.
7. **Given** a caller offers one customer identifier and immediately corrects it, **When** they
   continue, **Then** verification proceeds normally: misspeaking is not an attack.
8. **Given** a caller offers a third distinct customer identifier in the same call, **When** the
   backend evaluates it, **Then** a risk signal is recorded and the call escalates, regardless of
   whether any value was correct.
9. **Given** a caller corrects their email twice and then offers a customer identifier, **When** the
   identifier is evaluated, **Then** it is not penalised: the allowance is per factor.

### User Story 3 - Goodwill credit requested within policy (Priority: P3)

A verified customer asks for a goodwill credit below the auto-approval threshold. Eligibility
passes, no abuse pattern is found, and the agent records the request as a ticket carrying the
charge, the amount and the reason, then tells the caller it has been requested. It cannot apply a
credit: the ledger is not writable from a call, and a person accepts or rejects the ticket
afterwards.

**Why this priority**: It demonstrates that policy can be evaluated in full on the call — the
ceilings, the entry cap and the abuse patterns all decide here — while the money itself moves only
under a human decision. That separation is what makes the escalation behaviour meaningful rather
than universal.

**Independent Test**: As a verified customer with a clean history, request a CHF 40 credit against a
named charge and confirm the agent says it has been requested rather than applied, a ticket exists
carrying the charge and the amount as structured properties, an audit event names the authorizing
rule, and the ledger is unchanged.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a verified customer on an active account, naming a specific charge with no open dispute
   and no risk signals, **When** they request a credit below the threshold, **Then** the rules permit
   it, a ticket is raised, and the agent states the amount and that it has been requested.
1a. **Given** CHF 400 of credits in the last 12 months, **When** the customer requests CHF 100,
   **Then** it is permitted, because the total lands on exactly CHF 500.
1b. **Given** CHF 401 of credits in the last 12 months, **When** the customer requests CHF 100,
   **Then** it is not permitted, because the total would reach CHF 501.
1c. **Given** any credit history, **When** the customer requests CHF 101, **Then** it is not
   permitted, because a single request may not exceed CHF 100.
1d. **Given** a CHF 20 charge with no credits against it, **When** the customer requests CHF 100,
   **Then** it is not permitted, because a credit may not exceed the charge it is applied to.
1e. **Given** the customer cannot name a specific charge, **When** they request goodwill, **Then**
   no request is raised and the agent works with them to identify the entry or escalates.
2. **Given** a request is raised, **When** the action completes, **Then** a ticket carrying the
   charge, the amount and the reason exists, an audit event names the authorizing rule and records
   that a person must still accept it, and the ledger is unchanged.
3. **Given** a request at or above the threshold, **When** eligibility is evaluated, **Then** no
   ticket is raised and the agent escalates for approval.
4. **Given** the caller asks when they will see the credit, **When** the agent answers, **Then** it
   says a colleague will review it and they will hear back, and never states that it has been
   applied, credited, or will appear on a next statement.
5. **Given** the same caller asks twice for the same credit against the same charge, **When** the
   second request is evaluated, **Then** the open ticket is returned rather than a second one
   raised — including across a redialled call, because the check is scoped to the company.
6. **Given** the CRM cannot be read, **When** a credit is requested, **Then** the request is refused
   rather than raised, because the ceiling is computed partly from open tickets and an unreadable
   ceiling is not an empty one.

### User Story 4 - Threshold-splitting and abuse detection (Priority: P4)

The same customer makes another small credit request, but several small credits have already been
granted within the rolling window. The cumulative evaluation triggers, and the agent escalates
instead of granting, without accusing the caller.

**Why this priority**: A per-request threshold with no cumulative view is trivially defeated; this
is the difference between a rule and a control.

**Independent Test**: Seed prior credits totalling CHF 460 within the last 12 months, request a
further CHF 80, and confirm the request is not granted, a risk signal is recorded, and an escalation
is created.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** prior credits in the last 12 months totalling CHF 460, **When** a further CHF 80 credit
   is requested, **Then** it is not granted automatically, because the total would reach CHF 540.
2. **Given** the cumulative rule triggers, **When** the agent responds, **Then** it says the request
   needs review, gives a neutral reason, and makes no accusation of fraud.
3. **Given** any high-risk result, **When** eligibility would otherwise pass, **Then** the high-risk
   result wins and the request escalates.
4. **Given** CHF 300 of credits already applied and two open requests of CHF 99 each awaiting a
   decision, **When** a further CHF 40 is requested, **Then** it is not permitted, because a request
   nobody has answered has already spoken for that headroom even though no money has moved.
5. **Given** two callers from the same company request credits on the same day, **When** the second
   is evaluated, **Then** the first caller's open request counts against the ceiling, because the
   ceiling belongs to the customer and not to the call.

### User Story 5 - Degradation, escalation, and transfer failure (Priority: P5)

Backing systems fail mid-call: the identity store, the invoice store, the payment lookup, the CRM,
or the transfer itself. In every case the agent says what it does and does not
know, never presents a failure as an answer, and never leaves the caller without a next step.

**Why this priority**: The demo's credibility rests on the failure paths, and every escalation
trigger routes through this behavior.

**Independent Test**: An automated test per dependency simulates its failure and asserts that the
agent distinguishes "cannot tell you right now" from a factual negative, and that the caller always
leaves with a ticket, a callback, or a transfer.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** any store or lookup is unavailable, **When** the agent needs it, **Then** it reports a
   temporary problem, never states or implies an unverified financial fact, and offers a next step.
2. **Given** a payment status of UNKNOWN or SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE, **When** the agent replies,
   **Then** it does not describe the payment as succeeded, failed, or missing.
3. **Given** an escalation is triggered, **When** the agent transfers, **Then** a ticket exists
   first and a concise structured handoff carries verified identity state, intent, facts gathered,
   actions attempted, and the escalation reason.
4. **Given** the transfer fails, **When** the agent recovers, **Then** the escalation is persisted,
   a callback is created, and the caller is told on the call exactly what happens next and when.
5. **Given** a mutation succeeded but a following step failed, **When** the call ends, **Then** the
   completed mutation is not repeated, the incomplete step is recorded, and a human is notified.
6. **Given** a duplicate post-call notification for the same conversation, **When** it is processed,
   **Then** nothing is created twice.
7. **Given** the CRM is unreachable, **When** a credit is requested or an allocation proposed,
   **Then** the agent refuses rather than proceeding, because the request would exist nowhere and a
   caller told a colleague will look at it would be told something untrue.
8. **Given** a tool returns an error, **When** the agent speaks, **Then** it describes no outcome of
   that call — no ticket, no review, no timeline — because the action did not happen.

### User Story 6 - Multilingual and adaptive conversation (Priority: P6)

Callers speak German, French, Italian, or English. The agent detects the language and stays in it
for the whole call, including tool-driven statements and escalation messaging, and adapts pace,
sentence length, vocabulary, and how often it confirms, based on how the conversation is actually
going. Preferences persist to the next call.

**Why this priority**: Required for the Swiss context and highly visible in the demo, but worthless
if the financial behavior underneath it is wrong.

**Independent Test**: Run the golden path in each of the four languages and confirm the agent never
drifts back to another language, renders amounts and dates per locale while keeping values exact,
and offers to slow down or simplify when the caller struggles.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a caller opens in one of the four languages, **When** the conversation proceeds,
   **Then** every agent turn including tool results and escalation messages is in that language.
1a. **Given** the inbound number matches a customer with a stored language preference, **When** the
   agent greets, **Then** it greets in that language, does not use the caller's name, and does not
   indicate that the number was recognised.
1b. **Given** the inbound number is unknown or has no stored preference, **When** the agent greets,
   **Then** it greets in German and switches on the caller's first utterance.
2. **Given** the caller asks to switch language, **When** they do, **Then** the agent switches and
   persists the preference.
3. **Given** signs the caller is struggling to follow, **When** the agent adapts, **Then** it slows
   down, shortens sentences, or offers a simpler explanation, and bases this only on the observed
   conversation, never on assumptions about the person.
4. **Given** amounts and dates are spoken, **When** rendered, **Then** they follow the locale's
   conventions and remain numerically exact.

### User Story 7 - Interaction record, memory, and metrics (Priority: P7)

Every call leaves a defensible trail: a stored transcript under a retention lifecycle, one bounded
per-customer summary that the next call loads, an audit event for every consequential action, and a
set of per-interaction metrics from which operational rates are derived.

**Why this priority**: It is what makes the system reviewable and improvable, and it can be built
after the behavior it records is correct.

**Independent Test**: Complete two calls for the same customer and confirm the second loads the
summary written by the first alongside freshly fetched open invoices, escalations, and CRM data;
that the summary stays within roughly 2,000 characters; that no verification answers appear in it;
and that metrics exist for both interactions.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a completed call, **When** post-call processing runs, **Then** transcript, structured
   metadata, and metrics are stored keyed by conversation, and the customer summary is updated with
   provenance back to the source conversations.
2. **Given** a customer summary, **When** it is inspected, **Then** it contains no verification
   answers, no date of birth, and no payment credentials, and it stays within roughly 2,000
   characters after regeneration.
2a. **Given** a verified caller, **When** the agent loads context, **Then** it fetches open invoices,
   open escalations, past escalations, and the latest CRM information from their authoritative
   sources, and uses the summary only for narrative and preferences.
2b. **Given** the summary disagrees with a live source, **When** the agent speaks, **Then** it states
   the live value and never the summarised one.
3. **Given** a consequential action, **When** it is audited, **Then** the record names action,
   previous state, new state, authorizing rule, relevant identifiers, timestamp, agent version,
   risk result, and whether human approval was required.
4. **Given** a set of completed interactions, **When** rates are derived, **Then** first-call
   resolution, autonomous resolution rate, escalation rate, average handling time, tool error rate,
   verification failure rate, credit issuance rate, blocked-credit rate, and language distribution
   can all be computed from stored metrics.

### User Story 8 - Address discrepancy raised with the caller (Priority: P8)

The payment record carries an address that does not match the one on file. Once the payment has been
confirmed as matching, and only then, the agent raises the discrepancy with the caller in plain
terms — did the company move, or is it a typo? — records what the caller says as evidence, tells them
a person will take care of the correction, and attaches it to the review the human already has open.
The agent never changes the address itself.

**Why this priority**: The golden path surfaces the mismatch, so the behavior must be defined, and
asking the caller is both better evidence and cheaper than any automatic reconciliation engine.

**Independent Test**: Run the golden path with the seeded address mismatch and confirm the agent
raises it only after the payment match, asks the moved-or-typo question, records the answer as
evidence on the escalation, promises human correction, and writes nothing to the address itself.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an address on the payment record differs from the one on file, **When** the payment has
   not yet been confirmed, **Then** the agent does not raise the discrepancy and does not reveal
   either value.
2. **Given** the payment is confirmed as a MATCH, **When** the agent continues, **Then** it tells the
   caller the address held against the payment differs from the one on file and asks whether the
   company has moved or whether it is a typo.
3. **Given** the caller answers, **When** the agent responds, **Then** the answer is recorded as
   evidence against the conversation, the agent says a person will take care of the correction, and
   no address field is changed by the agent.
4. **Given** the escalation ticket already exists for the allocation review, **When** the discrepancy
   is recorded, **Then** it is attached to that same ticket rather than creating a second one.
5. **Given** any identity or financial field, **When** a conflict is found, **Then** the backend
   value stands and is never overwritten from the CRM or from what the caller says.

### Edge Cases

- A caller passes verification for one company but asks about an invoice belonging to another
  customer: ownership is checked server-side and the request is refused.
- A caller is verified as a person but has no authority to act for the company on the account.
- The caller's supplied payment details are partially right (correct amount, date outside the
  tolerance): the comparison returns NO_MATCH rather than being resolved in the caller's favour, and
  the agent invites them to re-check rather than accepting the near miss.
- The caller gives a date later than the record's: NO_MATCH, because the tolerance is backward-only
  and a payment cannot post before it was sent.
- The caller offers an approximate date ("some time last week"): the agent does not submit it, and
  asks for the exact date instead.
- Two unallocated payments of the same amount exist: the agent does not guess which one applies.
- The caller hangs up between a mutation and the confirmation turn.
- The caller switches language mid-sentence, or two people speak on the line.
- Background noise or a poor line makes an answer ambiguous: the agent re-asks rather than accepting
  a low-confidence match as a verification factor.
- A caller asks the agent to repeat a value it never disclosed, hoping to elicit it.
- The 12-month credit window boundary is crossed between two requests in the same call, or an older
  credit ages out mid-call and changes the answer.
- A dependency is slow rather than down: the caller has already heard the agent's acknowledgement, and
  at the 5-second timeout the agent says it cannot get an answer right now rather than waiting on.
- Post-call processing arrives out of order, twice, or not at all.

### User Story 9 - A human decision reaches the ledger (Priority: P9)

A colleague opens a ticket the agent raised, decides it, and sets the outcome. A scheduled applier
picks it up, re-reads the ledger, re-runs the rules against the account as it stands now, and
writes only if they still pass. It applies both kinds of request: a credit note, and an allocation
moving a payment against the invoices it settles.

It is the only component permitted to write the financial ledger and the only one a caller cannot
reach. No agent-facing role holds a write on the ledger under any circumstances, and IAM denies it
explicitly rather than relying on no Allow having been granted.

**Why this priority**: It is the only path by which money moves at all. Everything the agent does
stops at a request, so without this no credit ever reaches an account. It is also where a human
decision is checked rather than trusted: a person accepting something they misread must not be able
to produce an entry the rules would refuse.

**Independent Test**: Raise a credit request and an allocation on separate calls, set Request
outcome to Accepted on both, wait for the applier, and confirm the credit note exists on the ledger
derived from the ticket id, the payment has moved to ALLOCATED against the invoice it settles, both
tickets carry a note saying they were applied, and running the applier again writes nothing.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a ticket marked Accepted, **When** the applier runs, **Then** the credit note is written
   to the ledger, negative, attached to the named charge, and marked as accepted by a person.
2. **Given** the same ticket, **When** the applier runs a second time, **Then** nothing is written,
   because the ledger entry id is derived from the ticket id and the conditional write refuses a
   duplicate.
3. **Given** a ticket marked Accepted whose amount now breaches a ceiling, because other credits
   landed between the call and the decision, **When** the applier runs, **Then** it is refused, a
   note explaining the refusal is written to the ticket, and no ledger entry is created.
3a. **Given** an allocation ticket marked Accepted, **When** the applier runs, **Then** the payment
   moves from UNALLOCATED to ALLOCATED against every invoice named in `related_entry_id`, which
   carries the payment and the invoices it settles as a list, and the move is conditional on the
   payment still being UNALLOCATED.
3b. **Given** an allocation ticket whose payment has already been allocated, **When** the applier
   runs, **Then** nothing is written, because the conditional write refuses it.
3c. **Given** an allocation ticket naming an invoice the payment does not cover, **When** the
   applier runs, **Then** it is refused and a note explaining why is written to the ticket. A
   person accepting a ticket is not evidence the arithmetic works.
4. **Given** a ticket marked Rejected or Canceled by customer, **When** the applier runs, **Then**
   nothing is written to the ledger.
5. **Given** a ticket closed without an outcome set, **When** the applier runs, **Then** nothing is
   written, because a decision that was never recorded is not an approval.
6. **Given** one malformed ticket among several, **When** the applier runs, **Then** the others are
   still applied and the failure is counted and logged.
7. **Given** any agent-facing role, **When** it attempts to write the ledger, **Then** IAM denies it,
   whatever the code attempted. The Deny is explicit and attached to every such role, so a future
   Allow added by someone who does not know this rule does not reopen the path.
8. **Given** the applier, **When** its permissions are inspected, **Then** it holds no route in from
   a call: no API Gateway route, no public endpoint, and an invocation path that starts inside AWS
   on a schedule.

### User Story 10 - Telling an unauthorised caller who to ask (Priority: P10)

Someone rings about a company's account and is not a listed contact. They cannot be verified and
will not be, however genuine they are. Today the agent tells them to have an authorised person add
them, and then cannot say who that person is — which leaves them with an instruction they cannot
follow. The agent may give the name of a contact on the account, and nothing else.

**Why this priority**: A rule that cannot be acted on is worse than no rule. A caller who works at
the company already knows who handles their invoices; refusing the name buys nothing and strands a
legitimate colleague. What must never be given is anything that helps impersonate that person.

**Independent Test**: Call as someone who is not a listed contact, name the company, ask who can
authorise you, and confirm you are given a name and nothing else — no email, no phone number, no
job title, no financial fact.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a caller who has failed verification or is not a listed contact, **When** they ask who
   can add them, **Then** the agent gives the name of a contact on the account.
2. **Given** the same caller, **When** the name is given, **Then** nothing else about that person is
   given: not an email, not a phone number, not a date of birth, not a job title, not a location.
3. **Given** the same caller, **When** they ask anything financial, **Then** they get nothing —
   naming a contact does not verify them and moves no gate.
4. **Given** a caller who names a company with no account, **When** they ask who to speak to,
   **Then** the answer does not distinguish that from a company whose contacts cannot be found, so
   the endpoint cannot be used to discover which companies are customers.
5. **Given** a caller works through several company names in one call, **When** the pattern is
   detected, **Then** a risk signal is recorded and the call is escalated.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Disclosure and verification

- **FR-001**: The system MUST NOT disclose any invoice, payment, balance, credit, dispute, or other
  financial fact before verification succeeds, regardless of what the caller asserts.
- **FR-002a**: The identity of the verified contact — which person, not only which account — MUST
  be recorded on the conversation, in the audit record, and in any handoff, so a human knows who
  they were speaking to.
- **FR-002**: The verification outcome MUST be decided in the secure backend and returned as one of
  VERIFIED, PARTIALLY_VERIFIED, FAILED, or LOCKED; the conversational layer MUST NOT decide it.
- **FR-003**: Verification MUST require three independent identifying factors, applied uniformly to
  every disclosure and every action; there is no reduced tier for routine inquiries and no elevated
  tier for high-risk ones. The count MUST remain configuration rather than a hard-coded literal.
- **FR-003a**: The factor pool MUST be the caller's own email, their own phone number, their own
  date of birth, and the customer identifier of the account. At least one confirmed factor MUST be
  personal to the caller — email, phone or date of birth — so that a caller producing only company
  facts has demonstrated familiarity with the business and nothing about who they are.
- **FR-003d**: Identity records MUST be held per person, not per company. Any contact listed
  against an account MUST be able to verify using their own details and reach that account. A caller
  matching no listed contact MUST NOT reach it, whatever they claim about who they work for.
- **FR-003e**: The customer identifier names a company and MUST NOT resolve a person on its own. It
  is checked against the account the resolved contact belongs to, so a caller who gives correct
  personal details but names a different company does not verify.
- **FR-003f**: The account opening year MUST NOT be a factor. It is a fact about the company that
  every employee shares, so it distinguishes nobody, and almost nobody recalls it — which wasted an
  exchange before reaching a question that could be answered.
- **FR-003g**: The residual limitation MUST be recorded rather than designed around: colleagues
  commonly know one another's email, phone and date of birth, so these factors cannot distinguish
  one listed contact from another. This is tolerable because every listed contact has identical
  access, so impersonating a colleague gains nothing. It is not tolerable for someone who has left
  the company, and the control there is removing them from the contact list. A possession factor —
  a code to the phone on file — is the correct fix and is out of scope (FR-048).
- **FR-003b**: A caller's name MUST NOT count as a verification factor. Callers volunteer it in the
  first sentence of an ordinary call, so crediting it would hand over a third of the bar for free.
  First name and surname MUST NOT be treated as independent facts either: anyone who knows one
  almost always knows the other, and counting them separately would hand over two thirds. The tool
  contract MUST provide no field for a name.
- **FR-004**: The system MUST NOT reveal or hint at an expected verification answer, and MUST NOT
  tell the caller which specific factor failed.
- **FR-005**: The system MUST help a caller locate the requested information (for example, naming
  the document that carries it) without disclosing the value.
- **FR-006**: Failed verification attempts MUST be counted per customer and per caller session, and
  exceeding the configured limit MUST produce LOCKED, a recorded risk signal, and escalation.
- **FR-007**: Ownership and authority to act on the account MUST be checked in the backend for every
  request touching a customer's records.
- **FR-007a**: Being listed as a contact of the account is what confers authority. Any listed
  contact may verify and act; a caller who matches no listed contact MUST fail verification like any
  other stranger, however plausibly they claim to work there.
- **FR-007b**: When a caller cannot be verified, the agent MUST explain that an authorised contact
  on the account can add them. It MAY give that contact's name, and MUST give nothing else about
  them — no email address, no telephone number, no role, no location. A colleague at the customer
  already knows who works in their accounts department, so the name carries little; the contact
  details are what would let an impersonation proceed.
- **FR-007c**: An unauthorised caller MUST receive no financial information of any kind, including
  whether the company has an account at all.
- **FR-006a**: The number of *distinct* values offered for each verification factor within a call
  MUST be counted server-side. A caller may correct themselves once — misspeaking is human — but
  offering a third distinct value for the same factor is enumeration, not correction, and MUST
  raise a risk signal and escalate.
- **FR-006b**: The count MUST be per factor. Correcting a mistyped email must not consume the
  allowance for the customer identifier, or an honest caller with an unusual surname is treated as
  an attacker.
- **FR-006c**: Attempted values MUST be recorded as salted hashes, never as values, so distinct
  attempts can be counted without retaining what a caller guessed (Principle IV).
- **FR-006d**: This check MUST be enforced in the backend. The agent may also notice a caller
  fishing and escalate sooner, but a model noticing is not a control.

### Financial behavior

- **FR-008**: All financial rules, authorization, ownership checks, abuse checks, and integrity
  constraints MUST be enforced in the backend, independently of the conversational layer.
- **FR-009**: The conversational layer MUST NOT mutate authoritative financial or identity records
  on its own judgement; every mutation MUST be a backend operation that re-validates its own
  preconditions.
- **FR-010**: Payment matching MUST be performed server-side by comparing caller-supplied details
  against the stored record and returning MATCH, NO_MATCH, or INSUFFICIENT; the stored values MUST
  NOT be returned to the conversational layer.
- **FR-010a**: MATCH MUST require an exact amount, with no tolerance, and a transfer date within the
  configured backward tolerance of the date on record. NO_MATCH MUST be returned when the amount
  differs, when the claimed date falls outside the tolerance, or when no candidate payment exists.
  INSUFFICIENT MUST be returned when the caller cannot supply both fields, or when the supplied
  details fit more than one candidate payment.
- **FR-010b**: The date tolerance MUST be backward-only: the caller's date may precede the recorded
  date by up to `payment_date_tolerance_days` calendar days (default 3), and MUST NEVER follow it. A
  payment cannot post before it was sent, so a later claimed date is a genuine mismatch rather than a
  banking artefact.
- **FR-010e**: The tolerance exists because the payer sees the date their transfer left their
  account while the record may hold the date it arrived; a Friday transfer posting on Monday must not
  cause a caller answering honestly to be told their payment cannot be found. The tolerance MUST be
  configuration.
- **FR-010j**: When more than one of the customer's invoices could be the one a caller means, the
  agent MUST establish which before matching anything. Two overdue invoices and a caller saying "I
  paid it" is ambiguity, and ambiguity MUST NOT be resolved by choosing. If it cannot be resolved,
  the call MUST escalate.
- **FR-010c**: The caller MUST NOT be asked for the payment's reference. It is held on the record,
  and a payer rarely knows what their accounts department wrote. The caller supplies amount and date
  only, which serve to prove knowledge of the payment rather than to allocate it.
- **FR-010f**: Every invoice MUST carry two external identifiers: a human-readable
  `invoice_number` of the form `INV-<year>-<sequence>`, sequential from `0001` each year, which the
  agent speaks and the document prints; and a `payment_reference`, a random 7-digit key that the
  customer is asked to quote on the transfer.
- **FR-010g**: A payment's reference MUST be classified against the invoice under discussion as
  EXACT, PROBABLE_TYPO, OTHER_INVOICE, ABSENT, or UNRELATED.
  - EXACT when it equals this invoice's `payment_reference` or its `invoice_number`.
  - OTHER_INVOICE when it resolves exactly to a different invoice by either identifier. This MUST
    block the match: the money is earmarked elsewhere.
  - PROBABLE_TYPO when it is within `reference_typo_max_distance` edit operations of this invoice's
    `payment_reference` **and** resolves to no existing invoice.
  - ABSENT when empty, UNRELATED otherwise.
- **FR-010h**: Fuzzy matching MUST apply only to `payment_reference`, never to `invoice_number`.
  Invoice numbers are sequential, so consecutive invoices differ by one character; fuzzy matching
  against them would classify a payment correctly referencing the next invoice as a typo of this one
  and propose allocating it to the wrong invoice, with amount and date appearing to agree. The
  payment reference is random over ten million values, so a mistyped key almost never resolves to a
  real invoice and a near miss that resolves to nothing is safely a typo.
- **FR-010i**: The caller MUST NOT be asked to confirm or supply either identifier as part of
  matching. `invoice_number` is spoken only to identify which invoice is being discussed.
- **FR-010j**: Payer name and payer address MUST NOT affect the match result. A payment may
  legitimately be made by a parent company, a group entity, or a third party acting for the
  customer. Both MUST be surfaced as informational flags on the escalation so the reviewing human
  sees them.
- **FR-010d**: MATCH MUST NOT be returned when the payment record is not in a settled state; a record
  that is pending, unknown, or unavailable MUST NOT be reported as matching in either direction.
- **FR-011**: Payment status MUST be one of SUCCEEDED, PENDING, FAILED, UNKNOWN, or
  SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE, and UNKNOWN and SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE MUST NEVER be reported to the caller as a
  settled outcome in either direction.
- **FR-012**: An allocation above the agent's authority MUST be recorded as a ticket carrying the
  payment's entry id, and MUST NOT change the ledger. The payment stays UNALLOCATED until a person
  accepts the proposal. A second caller asking about the same payment MUST be told it is already in
  process, found from the open ticket rather than from the payment's status. When a person accepts
  the ticket, the applier moves the payment to ALLOCATED against the invoices named on it.
- **FR-013**: A goodwill credit MUST be requested only when every one of these holds: the caller is
  verified; the account is active and not in collections; the claim names a specific existing ledger
  entry; that entry has no open dispute; and no risk or abuse signal triggers. The conversational
  layer MUST NOT substitute its own judgement for any of these checks. The agent MUST NOT write the
  credit: it records a request, and MUST tell the caller it has been requested and never that it has
  been applied.
- **FR-013a**: The request MUST name the ledger entry it attaches to, so that every credit has an
  anchor that can be reconciled afterwards. A credit with no identified entry MUST NOT be requested.

- **FR-013e**: The rolling ceiling MUST count credits already applied to the ledger together with
  requests still awaiting a decision. A request nobody has answered has spoken for that headroom.

- **FR-013f**: When the ceiling cannot be read in full, the request MUST be refused rather than
  raised. An unreadable ceiling is not an empty one.

- **FR-012a**: An allocation ticket MUST carry the payment's entry id and the invoices it settles
  as a list, so the applier knows what to allocate and to what without re-deriving it.

- **FR-013g**: Only one principal MUST be able to write the financial ledger, and it MUST NOT be
  reachable from a call. It MUST re-read the ledger and re-run the credit rules before writing, and
  MUST refuse anything the rules refuse regardless of who accepted it. Its ledger entry id MUST be
  derived from the ticket, so applying twice writes once.
- **FR-013b**: Two ceilings MUST both hold, and both are inclusive. The requested credit MUST be at
  most CHF 100: a request of CHF 100 passes and CHF 101 does not. The customer's total credits over
  the rolling 12 months, counting the requested one, MUST be at most CHF 500: prior credits of
  CHF 400 plus a CHF 100 request passes at exactly CHF 500, while prior credits of CHF 401 plus a
  CHF 100 request does not.
- **FR-013c**: Prior credits against the same ledger entry MUST NOT disqualify a further credit in
  themselves; they constrain only through the customer's 12-month total and through FR-013d.
- **FR-013d**: A credit MUST NOT exceed the amount of the ledger entry it is credited against, less
  any credits already applied to that entry. This is a data-integrity rule, enforced independently of
  the policy ceilings in FR-013b, so that a customer can never be credited more than they were
  charged.
- **FR-014**: Credits MUST be evaluated cumulatively over a rolling 12-month window, with an
  inclusive ceiling of CHF 500 per customer. A request that would carry the customer's 12-month
  credit total above CHF 500 MUST NOT be granted automatically, even when the request itself is
  within the per-request limit. Both the ceiling and the window length MUST be configuration.
- **FR-015**: A high-risk result MUST override a passing eligibility result and force escalation.
- **FR-016**: Risk signals MUST include at least high contact frequency, repeated disputes, repeated
  failed verification, conflicting identity data, and unusual payment behavior, each recorded with
  type, timestamp, evidence, and conversation.
- **FR-017**: Balances MUST be derived from ledger entries rather than stored as an independent
  mutable figure.
- **FR-017a**: A payment exceeding the invoice it settles leaves a surplus on the account. The
  surplus MUST be offset against the customer's next invoice by default, and the agent MUST say so
  when asked rather than implying the money is lost or that a refund is automatic.
- **FR-017b**: A caller who asks for the surplus to be returned rather than offset is requesting a
  refund. It MUST be evaluated under the same limits as any other outbound amount: at most CHF 100
  in a single request and at most CHF 500 across the rolling twelve months, both inclusive. Above
  either limit it MUST escalate.
- **FR-017c**: The agent MUST NOT promise a refund, a timeline, or an offset amount it has not been
  told by the backend.

### Escalation and handoff

- **FR-018**: The system MUST escalate on any of: identity not established, repeated verification
  failure, disputed invoice validity, unreliable payment state, unresolved high-risk conflict,
  credit above authority, detected abuse, out-of-authority request, unrecoverable backend error,
  explicit request for a human, or a policy review requirement.
- **FR-019**: A ticket MUST be created before transfer, and a concise structured handoff MUST carry
  verified identity state, intent, facts gathered, actions attempted and their outcomes, and the
  escalation reason, so the caller does not repeat themselves.
- **FR-019a**: The transfer destination MUST be a single configured telephone number, changeable
  without redeploying, so that the failure path is exercised by repointing the configuration rather
  than by a demo-only code branch.
- **FR-019b**: When identity cannot be established, the agent MUST record what the caller came
  about, in the caller's own words, and transfer to a human with that context. The caller MUST NOT
  be made to repeat their problem to the person who takes over merely because the system could not
  confirm who they were.
- **FR-019c**: An unverified handoff MUST carry: the caller's stated problem; anything they claimed
  about themselves, explicitly labelled as unverified; the verification outcome and how many
  attempts were made; the language of the call; and the conversation identifier. It MUST NOT carry
  any invoice, payment, balance, credit, or account fact, because none was disclosed and none has
  been established as theirs.
- **FR-019d**: An unverified escalation MUST create a ticket that is not associated with any
  contact or company, since no customer has been established. Associating it on the caller's claim
  alone would write an unverified identity into the CRM.
- **FR-019e**: Caller-supplied text in an unverified handoff MUST be treated as untrusted content:
  recorded and shown to the human, never interpreted as an instruction by any downstream system.
- **FR-019f**: The agent MUST NOT tell the caller which detail failed, or that their answers were
  wrong. It says only that it cannot confirm their identity and that a colleague will take over
  (FR-004).
- **FR-020**: A failed transfer MUST NOT abandon the caller. The escalation and the callback MUST be
  persisted **before** the transfer is attempted, so the caller is covered whether or not the agent
  survives the failure. Where the agent does retain the call, it MUST tell them what happens next and
  within what timeframe.
- **FR-020a**: The design MUST NOT depend on the agent regaining control after a failed dial. The
  platform does not document that behaviour, and a promise that rests on undocumented behaviour is
  not a promise.

### Reliability

- **FR-022**: Every mutating operation MUST carry an idempotency key, and a repeated call with the
  same key MUST NOT produce a second effect.
- **FR-023**: Every tool call MUST have a hard timeout of 5 seconds. Idempotent read operations MAY
  be retried once with backoff; mutating operations MUST NOT be retried automatically. Both the
  timeout and the retry bound MUST be configuration.
- **FR-023a**: The agent MUST speak a short natural acknowledgement before invoking a tool, so that
  backend latency is covered by conversation rather than by silence. The system MUST NOT rely on a
  silence timer or filler audio to avoid dead air.
- **FR-024**: Post-call processing MUST validate the incoming notification and use the conversation
  identifier as its idempotency key, so duplicates create nothing twice.
- **FR-025**: A partial failure after a completed financial or CRM mutation MUST leave the completed
  part intact, record the incomplete part, and notify a human.
- **FR-026**: Tool responses MUST be structured statuses; a failure MUST be distinguishable by the
  conversational layer from an authoritative negative answer.

### Data boundaries

- **FR-027**: Identity data and authoritative financial data MUST reside only in the secure backend.
- **FR-028**: The CRM MUST hold only name, company, contact identifier, owner, commercial metadata,
  interaction records, tickets, and escalation status, and MUST NEVER receive date of birth,
  verification answers, payment credentials, or financial detail beyond what a ticket needs.
- **FR-029**: Logs, transcripts summaries, and conversation summaries MUST NOT contain verification
  answers or raw sensitive identity fields.
- **FR-030**: The backend identity store MUST be authoritative for identity, the backend financial
  stores for financial state, and the CRM only for CRM metadata; conflicts MUST resolve in favour of
  the authoritative source.
- **FR-031**: A non-authoritative profile conflict, such as a mismatched address, MUST be detected
  and recorded with the value each source held, and MUST be resolved by a human. The agent MUST NOT
  correct the field itself.
- **FR-031a**: The agent MUST raise an address discrepancy with the caller only after the associated
  payment has been confirmed, MUST ask whether the company moved or the record carries a typo, MUST
  record the caller's answer as evidence against the conversation, and MUST state that a person will
  make the correction.
- **FR-031b**: A discrepancy found during a call that already has an escalation MUST be attached to
  that escalation rather than creating a second one.
- **FR-032**: All data used MUST be synthetic.

### Language and interaction

- **FR-033**: The system MUST support German, French, Italian, and English, detect the caller's
  language, and continue in it for every turn including tool-driven statements and escalation
  messaging.
- **FR-033a**: The opening greeting MUST use the stored language preference associated with the
  inbound caller number when one is found, and German otherwise. The agent MUST switch immediately
  if the caller's first utterance is in another language.
- **FR-033b**: Recognising an inbound number MUST select the greeting language only. The agent MUST
  NOT greet the caller by name, acknowledge that the number is known, or otherwise confirm anything
  about the account to an unverified caller. Number recognition is never a verification factor and
  never counts toward the three required factors.
- **FR-034**: The system MUST adapt pace, sentence length, vocabulary, explanation depth, and
  confirmation frequency to the observed course of the conversation, and MUST offer to slow down,
  simplify, or switch language.
- **FR-035**: Adaptation MUST NOT be based on inferences about education, nationality, ethnicity, or
  cognitive ability.
- **FR-034a**: The agent MUST match the caller's conversational register — brisk with a caller who
  wants only the answer, warmer with a caller who converses — while remaining courteous and concise
  in both. Register MUST be read from how the caller speaks, never from their name, accent, company,
  or origin.
- **FR-034b**: The agent MUST NOT mirror hostility. An annoyed caller MUST be met with a level tone,
  one acknowledgement that the problem is real, and progress toward a fix.
- **FR-034c**: Adaptation MUST apply only to how something is said. It MUST NOT affect what may be
  disclosed, what the backend has decided, or what the agent is authorised to do. A caller's warmth
  MUST NOT move the verification gate or any policy limit, because rapport is a route to disclosure
  and an agreeable caller is not a verified one.
- **FR-036**: Preferred language and communication style MUST be persisted and loaded on the next
  call.
- **FR-037**: Monetary amounts, dates, and reference numbers MUST be rendered per locale convention
  and remain exact.

### Memory, audit, and metrics

- **FR-038**: Raw transcripts MUST be stored per customer and conversation and deleted after 90 days.
- **FR-038a**: Retention MUST be applied per record class and MUST be configuration: raw transcripts
  90 days; conversation metadata and per-interaction metrics 10 years; the customer summary until 12
  months after account closure; audit events 10 years. Expiry MUST be enforced by the storage
  lifecycle rather than by application code remembering to delete.
- **FR-039**: Exactly one interaction summary MUST exist per customer, covering recent billing
  issues, unresolved disputes, outcomes, language and communication preferences, company commitments,
  prior resolution steps, and permitted risk patterns, with provenance to its source conversations.
- **FR-039a**: After each call the summary MUST be regenerated by summarising the prior summary
  together with the call just completed, and MUST be capped at approximately 2,000 characters.
- **FR-039b**: The summary is narrative context only and MUST NOT be relied on for authoritative
  state. On successful identification the agent MUST load, live from the authoritative sources: open
  invoices, open escalations, past escalations, and the latest CRM information. Where the summary and
  a live source disagree, the live source stands.
- **FR-039c**: Because regeneration can lose or reword detail, no financial statement to a caller MUST
  ever rest on the summary alone.
- **FR-040**: On call completion the system MUST persist the raw record, load the summary, update it,
  and persist it again.
- **FR-041**: Every consequential automated action MUST be audited with action, previous state, new
  state, authorizing rule, relevant identifiers, timestamp, agent version, risk result, and whether
  human approval was required.
- **FR-042**: Per-interaction metrics MUST record conversation, customer, agent identity and version,
  timestamps, duration, language, verification duration/result/attempts, tools invoked with counts,
  latency, failures, retries, financial actions attempted and completed, credits issued, escalation
  required and reason, transfer attempted and result, final outcome, autonomous resolution flag,
  CRM ticket identifier, and error category.
- **FR-043**: First-call resolution, autonomous resolution rate, escalation rate, average handling
  time, tool error rate, verification failure rate, credit issuance rate, blocked-credit rate, and
  language distribution MUST be derivable from stored metrics.
- **FR-044**: Every relevant interaction MUST be logged in the CRM, and every escalation MUST create
  a ticket linked to the contact and company.

### Policy configuration and security

- **FR-045**: Credit limits, abuse windows, required verification factor counts, confidence
  thresholds, retry bounds, timeouts, and retention periods MUST be configuration, changeable
  without editing agent instructions or redeploying business logic.
- **FR-046**: Tool endpoints MUST be authenticated, all tool input MUST be validated server-side,
  access MUST be least-privilege, sensitive stores MUST be encrypted at rest, and secrets MUST live
  in a secrets manager and never in the repository.

### Scope boundaries

- **FR-047**: Inbound calls MUST be answerable on a real provisioned telephone number, so the demo
  is a genuine inbound phone call rather than a browser session.
- **FR-048**: Outbound payment links and outbound messaging (SMS or otherwise) are OUT OF SCOPE.
  No journey depends on them, and the transfer-failure fallback MUST be self-contained within the
  call and the ticket rather than relying on an outbound channel.
- **FR-049**: Automatic reconciliation of conflicting profile fields — confidence scoring,
  threshold-gated auto-correction, and a standalone review queue — is OUT OF SCOPE. Conflict
  detection, caller-sourced evidence, and human correction via the existing escalation cover the
  need at a fraction of the cost.

### Key Entities *(include if feature involves data)*

- **Customer identity record**: customer identifier, first and last name, date of birth, postal
  address, phone, email, verification metadata, account status, account opening date, optional risk
  indicators. Lives only in the secure backend.
- **Ledger entry**: a single record type covering all customer financial entries — entry identifier,
  customer, type (invoice, payment, credit note, adjustment), date, due date for invoices, signed
  amount, currency, status (open, overdue, paid, unallocated, under review, allocated, pending
  approval, approved, rejected), reference, entries it is allocated to, reason, originating
  conversation, decision source, approval status. Balance is derived from these.
- **Risk signal**: customer, signal type, timestamp, evidence, originating conversation.
- **Conversation record**: conversation identifier, customer, transcript reference, structured
  metadata, per-interaction metrics, outcome, escalation status, agent version, language.
- **Customer summary**: one bounded per-customer digest carrying billing history, unresolved
  disputes, preferences, open escalations, commitments, and provenance — no verification answers.
- **Audit event**: action, previous state, new state, authorizing rule, identifiers, timestamp, agent
  version, risk result, human-approval flag.
- **Escalation ticket**: reason, structured handoff context, linked contact and company, status.
- **CRM contact and company**: name, company, contact identifier, owner, commercial metadata,
  interaction records, tickets, escalation status. No sensitive identity or financial detail.
- **Policy configuration**: credit threshold, rolling abuse window and cumulative limit, required
  factor counts by risk level, confidence thresholds, retry bounds, timeouts, retention periods.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: In 100% of attempts, no financial fact is disclosed before verification succeeds,
  including under direct assertion of identity, claimed urgency, and repeated pressure.
- **SC-002**: No identity field beyond name, company, and contact identifier ever appears in the CRM,
  verified by inspecting every record the system writes.
- **SC-003**: No authoritative financial record changes without a backend rule authorizing it; 100%
  of financial mutations carry an audit event naming that rule.
- **SC-004**: The golden path completes end to end in each of German, French, Italian, and English,
  with no turn falling out of the caller's language.
- **SC-005**: Every one of the required failure scenarios — identity store down, invoice store down,
  payment lookup down, CRM down, transfer failure, duplicate post-call notification, partial failure
  after a mutation — is covered by an automated test that simulates the failure and asserts a
  caller-safe outcome with a next step and no fabricated financial statement. These scenarios are
  verified by the test suite rather than demonstrated on a live call.
- **SC-006**: A credit below the threshold with a clean history is resolved on the call without a
  human; the same request with prior credits inside the rolling window is blocked and escalated.
- **SC-007**: Replaying any mutating operation or post-call notification produces no duplicate
  record, verified for allocation proposals, credits, tickets, and post-call persistence.
- **SC-008**: A second call for the same customer loads the summary written by the first and also
  fetches open invoices, open escalations, past escalations, and CRM data live; the summary stays
  within roughly 2,000 characters across at least ten successive regenerations.
- **SC-009**: Every completed interaction has metrics from which all nine derived rates can be
  computed.
- **SC-010**: The golden path is demonstrable in 3–5 minutes, and the whole build fits roughly 20
  hours of work.

## Assumptions

- The company, its customers, invoices, payments, and all data are fictional and synthetic; no real
  personal or financial data is used anywhere.
- The conversational and orchestration layer is ElevenLabs; the secure identity and financial
  backend runs on AWS; the CRM is a real HubSpot developer account. These are fixed platform
  constraints from the brief rather than open design choices.
- Callers are business contacts acting for a customer company, so authority to act for the company
  matters alongside personal identity.
- Requiring an exact amount and a near-exact transfer date means some callers must open their banking
  app mid-call. That friction is accepted deliberately: it is the property that stops a caller holding
  a stolen invoice from matching a payment, since neither the transfer date nor the exact transferred
  amount appears on the invoice document. The 3-day tolerance widens the guessable date space from
  one day to four, which is acceptable because the amount remains exact.
- Language preference is keyed to the inbound caller number, which is a convenience only. A caller
  on an unrecognised line is not disadvantaged: they are greeted in German and switched on their
  first words.
- The human side of an escalation is a single "billing specialist" line, in practice the builder's
  own mobile. There is no queue, no routing, and no voicemail; an unanswered or unroutable transfer
  falls through to the persisted-escalation-plus-callback path.
- Inbound telephony uses an existing provisioned number (the provisioned inbound number). The number is US-based
  while the fictional company is Swiss; this is accepted as a demo artifact and is not presented as
  the company's real service line. Call transfer therefore has a real dial path available.
- The demo runs against seeded fixtures: a disputing customer with an overdue CHF 4,200 invoice and
  a matching unallocated payment carrying a mismatched address; a clean customer for the autonomous
  credit; and a customer with prior small credits inside the window for the abuse case.
- Verification is a single uniform bar of three factors. Risk is handled after verification, by the
  abuse and eligibility rules and by escalation, rather than by a second verification tier — which
  keeps the golden path short enough for the Loom without weakening the gate.
- Goodwill credit policy starts at an inclusive CHF 100 per request and an inclusive CHF 500
  cumulative per rolling 12 months;
  allocation authority sits below CHF 4,200, so the golden path escalates by policy rather than by
  accident.
- A 12-month window is deliberately long: it makes threshold-splitting impractical rather than merely
  slower, at the cost of a legitimately unlucky customer waiting longer for the ceiling to reset.
  That customer is escalated to a human rather than refused.
- Human validation of a proposed allocation happens outside this system; the promise to the caller
  is a 24-hour resolution target, not an automated completion.
- A single agent version is deployed at a time; the version is recorded on every audit event and
  metric so behavior can be attributed after a change.
- Regenerating the summary with a language model each call risks drift, which is accepted only
  because no financial fact depends on it: open invoices, escalations, and CRM data are fetched live
  on every call. The summary's job is to spare the caller from repeating context, not to hold truth.
- Metadata and metrics share the audit trail's 10-year life rather than a shorter analytics-style
  window, so that a financial action taken on a call can still be explained from the record a decade
  later. Only the raw transcript, the most sensitive artefact, expires quickly.
- Failure scenarios are proven by automated tests that simulate each dependency failing. There is no
  runtime fault-injection switch and no live teardown, so the Loom shows the golden path and refers
  to the test suite for the failure behaviour rather than triggering an outage on camera. This trades
  demo persuasiveness for build time, which the 20-hour budget favours.
- Transfer failure is the exception: it is exercised live simply by repointing the destination number
  at an unroutable value, which needs no fault-injection machinery.
- Voice quality, accent handling, and telephony audio conditions are accepted as provided by the
  platform and are not tuned as part of this work.
